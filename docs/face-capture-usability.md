# Face capture: quick assisted search and guided verification

The three editions share the same on-device face module and camera handling, with separate capture paths for assisted search and live verification.

- Pressing **Start camera** begins routine student lookup automatically when camera frames and the model are ready; no separate capture click is needed. Lookup takes a straight-facing sample after three steady, good-quality frames. No head turn or blink is required. Only one sufficiently large, centred, unclipped face is accepted, with a finite 1024-value descriptor. This is **not a live-person check**, does not authenticate anyone, and is less resistant to a photograph than movement-checked capture. Staff must visually confirm every possible match before opening the record or continuing an action. Existing encrypted enrollments and match thresholds are unchanged.
- A scan stops the camera after a sample is captured. No-match and network-failure results let staff start a fresh scan. If framing times out while the stream is still active, **Retry scan** starts another attempt. Switching an idle active camera automatically scans with the newly selected camera. Capture locks and stream-generation checks prevent duplicate or stale scans. Enrollment remains explicitly initiated with **Capture face**, and attendance keeps its signed movement checks.
- Assisted search defaults to the back camera. Staff clock-in/out and enrollment default to the front camera. Both camera choices remain available in every flow. A supported physical camera can also be selected after camera permission. Switching cameras resets the capture stream; closing the dialog, changing streams or losing the track cancels in-progress capture.
- Guided capture no longer depends on catching a split-second blink. New attendance challenges randomly choose a small left or right head turn, then require a return to centre. Enrollment uses a left turn. Older signed blink challenges remain supported with a slower close/open-eyes instruction.
- Guided calibration uses three stable frames and accepts natural eye-opening differences and a modest phone angle. The captured face still needs sufficient confidence, size, visible boundaries, the requested live action, and a frontal return before a descriptor can be collected. Only legacy blink challenges need an open-eye baseline.
- Portrait guides retain a 0.76 width/height ratio. Size checks compare face width to video width and face height to video height; a tall portrait face is not incorrectly rejected against the camera's width.
- Camera/audio controls are outside the preview. One status message gives the current instruction. Capture is the main button once the camera is ready; retries reuse the stream. Preview height adapts to the viewport and the footer remains reachable on small screens.
- Head-turn directions follow the bundled Human 3.3.6 rotation convention: negative yaw is left, positive yaw is right. A regression test exercises the actual library pose calculation, rather than assuming the signs in simulated camera frames. CSS mirroring of the front-camera preview does not change the measured direction.
- Human error results are surfaced immediately instead of being mistaken for a missing face. An empty or unusable student gallery explains that enrollment is required; deleted enrollments are not restored automatically.
- Records Desk queries finance only for the selected student's references, with paginated, bounded reads. A finance failure shows an unavailable notice without partial or zero totals and does not block the permitted profile or face-enrollment action. Branch, section and legacy-identity checks still apply before any activity is displayed.
- Signed attendance challenges, account matching, enrollment device-unlock proof, encrypted templates and existing match thresholds are unchanged. No raw camera frame is uploaded or stored.

Automated tests exercise quick lookup without movement, both camera orientations, poor/invalid samples, interrupted steady frames, cancellation, simulated slow-camera guided capture, natural narrow eyes, portrait sizing, legacy blinks, left/right challenges and wrong/no movement. Physical camera accuracy and spoken instruction timing still need a check on the affected handset; these tests do not establish bank-grade anti-spoofing accuracy.
