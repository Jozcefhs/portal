# Hospital theatre, surgery and procedure workflows

Development increment: 27 October 2026. Web companion and shared backend on `codex/hospital-edition`. Test and push only; no deployment, compilation or installer release. All verification uses synthetic records.

## Staff workflow

1. Enable **Theatre and procedures** for the Hospital subscription and relevant staff. It depends on patient registration and reception and is included in Hospital Standard and above. Hospital Administrators/Super Admins configure rooms without access to clinical procedure records. Doctors/Medical Directors book and sign procedures; Nurses participate in preparation, clinical reading, preoperative checks and recovery handover.
2. In **Theatres and preparation**, an administrator creates each room with a permanent code and editable name/active state. A clinical officer explicitly confirms preparation before first use and after a completed procedure. An occupied theatre cannot be deactivated. Renames and preparation confirmations retain versions.
3. A Doctor or Medical Director opens a patient's existing Reception visit and selects **Plan procedure**. Load theatres, select a room and enter a permanent procedure reference, planned procedure, site/side, lead clinician's staff username, team and booking times in WAT. The lead must be an active Doctor/Medical Director assigned to the branch. Both outpatient and admitted visits are supported; registration, visit, NFC identity and existing clinical records are reused.
4. Bookings last at most 24 hours, are entered to the minute and can start within one year. The room, patient and lead clinician cannot have overlapping reservations. Patient and lead-clinician checks span branches within the Hospital workspace. Staff can reschedule an unstarted booking with a reason; previous booking details remain in history, and a previous preoperative review must be repeated. An unstarted case can be cancelled with a reason; its record remains and reserved times are released. Cancellation cannot erase a started procedure.
5. Record **Preoperative checks** with the consent-record reference, preparation and anaesthetic assessment. Confirm patient identity, procedure/site, consent, allergies and team preparation. This records staff checks; it does not generate consent, verify an uploaded consent document or determine clinical fitness. Changed allergy information requires a fresh preoperative review.
6. The named lead clinician or Medical Director confirms the team time-out and starts the procedure within its booked interval. If delayed beyond that interval, reschedule before starting. The theatre must be active, prepared and unoccupied. Actual occupancy also protects the patient and lead clinician: a procedure that overruns its reservation still prevents them from being started elsewhere. Other team members and equipment are recorded as text, not individually reserved resources.
7. Sign the operative record: procedure performed, diagnosis, findings, technique, anaesthesia/monitoring references, counts/specimens, implants/consumables references, complications/outcome and recovery plan. These are practitioner-entered records, not generated treatment advice. An aborted or changed procedure is documented through its actual operative outcome and recovery/handover, retaining the original plan.
8. Record recovery observations, disposition/destination, receiving officer and handover details. Confirm the patient has left the theatre and handover occurred. This completes the case, releases actual occupancy and outstanding reservations, and marks the theatre as needing preparation. It does not automatically discharge the patient, acknowledge receipt on behalf of another officer or change the patient's ward bed. The existing inpatient/nursing handover and discharge workflows remain available.
9. Review or amend signed notes through the procedure record. Only the relevant author or Medical Director may amend them, with a reason; operative amendments additionally require clinical signing permission. Original notes and every revision remain in dated history. Amendments are allowed after visit closure without reopening the visit, creating another charge or rewriting original signed events.

## Billing, medicines and stock

- The completed case displays its permanent procedure reference. In the patient's **Billing → Add a service charge**, choose **Procedure** and enter that reference in **Completed procedure reference**, with the service code, description, quantity, tariff and financial reference. The server validates the completed case against the same patient, visit and branch.
- The charge identity includes the procedure and service code, preventing another charge for the same service on fresh clicks as well as request retries. Identical repeats make no writes; changed details require correction through the existing financial workflow. Different service codes can represent distinct services within the same procedure.
- Charges feed the existing invoice, patient/HMO/corporate coverage, payment, credit and accounting workflows. Theatre booking, clinical signing, cancellation, recovery and amendments do not automatically create charges, payments, invoices, refunds or journals. Cancellation does not silently erase previous finance records.
- **Open care record** and **Prescriptions and investigations** keep work on the same visit. Medicines continue through the existing prescribing, pharmacy dispensing and administration workflows; those workflows own stock and charge entries. Operative notes retain staff-entered medicine/specimen/stock/batch references. Saving a theatre note does not deduct stock or bill the patient again.
- Generic surgical consumable issue/return, automatic implant inventory, sterile-tray tracking and automatic procedure tariffs are still pending. Free-text consumable or anaesthetic references are documentation, not automatic inventory movements or medication administration records. Card-terminal payments remain disabled.

## Record integrity and concurrency

- Each mutation commits the case/current state, immutable event, audit, request receipt and applicable visit/resource guards atomically, with current-document preconditions. Conflicting changes fail without partial reservations, occupancy or clinical records.
- Permanent theatre codes and visit-specific procedure references prevent duplicate setup/cases. Request receipts handle lost responses and repeat submissions; reusing a request ID with different content is rejected. Unchanged setup, booking, preoperative review and amendment saves create no audit/event/receipt writes.
- Exact resource/day reservation documents prevent overlap without searching historical bookings. Reservations are bounded to 96 entries per resource/day. At most two WAT dates are involved in one booking. Rescheduling reads each distinct old/new resource/day once. Separate actual-occupancy records protect the patient and lead clinician during overruns; the room retains its active-case reference until recovery handover.
- Scheduled/in-progress cases contribute to a visit's pending-procedure counter. Reception closure and inpatient discharge require outstanding procedures to be completed or cancelled explicitly. Booking and visit closure compete on the same revision, so a race cannot leave a new booking on a closed visit. Theatre work invalidates earlier discharge approval while retaining signed summaries. The admission reference is captured from the current visit at procedure start, including admission after booking.
- Hospital edition, module, subscription, role, branch, patient and revision checks apply server-side. Administrative room lists expose readiness/occupied status, without patient identity or procedure notes. Clinical registers are branch-scoped. A cross-branch scheduling conflict does not expose the other branch's patient or case.
- Backup collection discovery includes `Theatres`, `TheatreVersions`, `TheatreReservations`, `TheatreActiveResources`, `ProcedureCases` and `ProcedureEvents`. No existing collection or production record is deleted, migrated or rewritten in bulk.

## Reads, writes and freshness

- Opening the workspace/tab loads no theatre data. Room loading is explicit. Procedure registers and event histories require dates, limited to 93 days, fetching 31/displaying 30 with timestamp/document-ID cursors. Registers carry saved patient/room/clinician labels and perform no per-row document lookups.
- Loaded pages are retained in a bounded 40-page memory cache. Opening a case keeps the loaded register available. Navigation and repeated loads reuse those pages; Refresh explicitly fetches newer data. Mutations invalidate related views. There is no polling, automatic full-history load or browser-storage persistence of clinical content.
- Current case/patient/visit/care context is read explicitly before significant actions; cached lists do not authorise mutations. Branch/session changes clear records and unsaved edits require a discard decision, including after rejected saves.
- Measured synthetic service budgets for a one-day case: booking uses nine exact reads, one clinician query limited to two rows and nine atomic writes; preoperative review uses five reads/six writes; starting uses eight reads/nine writes; recovery completion uses eleven reads/eleven writes. These include operation receipts and audit. Register pages use one bounded query and one access audit with no patient reads. Completed mutation retries use one receipt read and no writes. Distinct resource reads run concurrently.
- These are application-operation budgets, not live Firestore billing measurements. Authentication, subscription/branch configuration, explicit UI context reloads and conflict retries are separate. Matching Firestore index definitions are included but not deployed.
